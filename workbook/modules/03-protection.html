{# ── Step 2: Term vs. permanent life insurance ───────────────── #}
Module 3 · Step 2 of {{ m.steps | length }}
{{ ui.step("m3_s2", m.steps[1][0]) }}

Life insurance may pay a lump sum to the people you choose if you pass away. There are two main kinds. A simple way to picture them: term covers a set stretch of years, permanent is meant to last your whole life.

{{ ui.compare("Term", "Permanent", [ ("How long it lasts", "A set number of years, for example 10 or 20", "Your whole life, as long as payments are kept up"), ("Cost to start", "Usually lower", "Usually higher"), ("When the years are up", "Coverage ends, or may be renewed at a new price", "Doesn’t end on a set date"), ("Often used for", "Years when others count on you, like raising kids or paying a mortgage", "Lifelong needs, like final costs or leaving something behind"), ]) }}

Neither one is “better.” Some families use one kind, some use the other, and some use both. Details vary a lot from policy to policy.
